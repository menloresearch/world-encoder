# Kepler encoder — progress

> **SUPERSEDED 2026-07-30 → see `PROGRESS.md`** (the single live tracker; kept for history).

## Goal

Test if the encoder makes a policy better on RoboCasa. Fair setup: same demos, same recipe,
matched checkpoints, 2 seeds.

## 1. Latent replaces the policy's vision ❌

Ran: policy trained on our latent instead of raw images.
Result: 32% → 14% (2% with the pretrained encoder).
Learned: the latent alone doesn't carry enough for control.

## 2. Latent added next to the policy's vision ❌

Ran: baseline policy + our latent as an extra input. Twice — first with the v0.2 encoder, then
with the better v0.3 encoder (better probes, more detail).
Result: v0.2 no change (24–28% vs 32–36%). v0.3 worse (16 vs 22 and 8 vs 32 at matched checkpoints).
Learned: better latent quality didn't help, so this way of feeding it in doesn't work. Also, our
probe metrics went up while success went down — offline metrics don't predict policy results.

## 3. Latent as a training signal (aux loss) ❌

Ran: extra loss pushing the policy to predict the encoder's future latent. Weight sweep
0.2 / 0.05 / 0.01 / 0.
Result: 4 / 10–14 / 26 / 24–26% success — more weight, worse policy. Training loss looked normal
throughout.
Learned: our loss target is frozen, so the loss can never reach zero and its gradient keeps
distorting the policy's vision features. Papers that made this work use a moving target and keep
the loss off the vision encoder. Could retry with that design later, low priority.

## 4. Force fusion — not run yet

Plan (E6): contact-rich sim tasks (Square / ToolHang / Coffee) with a wrist force sensor.
Compare: no force / raw force readings / force fused through our encoder / contact-gated fusion.
The fused latent has to beat raw readings, not just the no-force baseline. Nobody in the
literature has cleared this bar yet — raw force is a strong baseline.

## What's next

Planned:

1. Run the force test (E6). If force fused through the encoder beats raw force readings, the
   encoder has a proven use. Main next step.
2. Rerun the no-encoder baseline (E2): frozen patches straight to the policy. Gives us the ceiling
   every result should be measured against.
3. One retry of the training-signal idea with the corrected design (moving target, loss off the
   vision encoder, gated weight). Low priority.

If force also fails, the open questions, in order of suspicion:

- **Architecture.** We pool everything into one 256-d vector, which destroys spatial structure.
  Published results say keep per-patch structure and compress channels instead. A redesign =
  spatial latents (per patch region) instead of pooled queries. Biggest suspect.
- **Where the latent is used.** Field results say compressed latents pay off in prediction and
  planning, not as inputs to a reactive policy. If E6 fails, the encoder's job moves there —
  though even in planning, patch-structured latents beat pooled ones, which points back to
  architecture.
- **Our metrics.** Partly known bad already: probes went up while success went down. We need
  offline checks that actually predict policy results; right now only rollouts do.
- **The loss.** Least suspect. We already fixed it once (v0.3 detail pressure) — metrics improved,
  policy didn't. A better loss alone doesn't rescue the reactive-policy uses; it may still matter
  for prediction uses.
