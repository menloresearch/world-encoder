## tl;dr — the short version

**the issue in one sentence.** the encoder is fine, every way we tried to feed its output to a policy is what failed, and we now know why. wave 1 settled it: our tokens next to the policys own vision tie the baseline (0.86 vs 0.90), so encoder quality is exonerated. the failures were three mechanisms that all look healthy in training and only show up in rollouts: concatenation corrupts the policys observation distribution at rollout (0.34), state conditioning makes the tokens actively harder to use (0.54), and the aux loss distorts the policys vision in proportion to its weight. on top of that we were testing in a regime where winning was near impossible: at 200 demos frozen encoders lose to end to end training by ~42 percent across the literature, every published frozen encoder win sits at 100 demos or fewer, and square is solved by vision alone so there was no headroom for anything extra to help.

**what we hope to prove.** the bet is that a small learned summary of what the robot senses is worth more than raw pixels somewhere that actually matters. the naive version (plug z into any policy and it gets better) is dead and proven dead properly. three sharper versions are live. one, robustness: a task driven compressed representation is what survives when the world changes, ties in distribution mean nothing and the ordering flips under shift. two, data efficiency: at 10 to 50 demos, the realistic amount anyone has for a new task on a real robot, pretrained compressed features beat learning from scratch, every published frozen win lives there. three, planning, the big one: 8 tokens are a sufficient world model state and planning in that space is 10 to 100x cheaper per planner call, the head to head against dense patches in manipulation is unpublished so we can be first, and planning is the one place compression is required, you cannot roll 600 patch tokens forward over long horizons. and we prove something either way: if all three miss, the rigorous negative (strongest baselines, preregistered gates, the literature independently agreeing) plus the methodological findings (offline metrics invert against rollout success, the three insertion harm mechanisms, the collapse failure mode and its detectors) stops the field from re running the same dead ends. the exoneration also protects v0.1: the papers claims were never undermined by the downstream nulls, and the surviving program runs on exactly v0.1s real assets, the force signal and the predictor.

**are we changing the encoder architecture.** yes, but scoped. the windowed multi modal rebuild is the machinery for planning and for a future multi rate force test, it does not reopen the feed z to a policy question, that is closed. it also gets collapse rails (nuisance detectors, a static pattern canary, a loss crossover monitor) because this objective family can silently collapse onto static predictable channels, like camera identity, while the training loss looks great.

**what changes about downstream testing.** new axes instead of new insertions: distribution shift (eval only, on checkpoints we already have), low demo counts, and z as world model state for planning. one last insertion pattern because it is the only one with a published win, the action head pulls from the tokens by cross attention instead of tokens being pushed into the observations. and process: preregistered pass or kill bars before any number is seen, kill at the earliest decisive read, verdicts in hours not days, offline metrics permanently kill only, only rollouts say works.

**best results so far.** v0.1: one encoder trained across 4 robots matches per robot specialists in domain and transfers where specialists dont, and its latent reads force better than raw vision features on every force sensored robot. the predictor beats carrying the latent forward by 28 to 36 percent at every horizon, replicated in a second domain, and it is exactly what the planning direction builds on. multicam fusion improves the force probe (0.283 vs 0.251) with no rank collapse, early fusion beats everything else. wave 1 parity (0.86 vs 0.90) is the exoneration result that redirected the whole program from fix the encoder to fix what you do with it. what we do not have yet: a single downstream lift, and the literature says that was always the expected outcome at our operating point. the three live shots cost about 5 to 7 gpu nights total to resolve.

## the problem

kepler is a perceiver style encoder, learnable queries cross attending frozen vit patch tokens plus robot state (later force), trained with masked cross modal latent prediction under the lejepa/sigreg objective. output is 8 tokens of width 256 versus roughly 600 patch tokens per camera. the program question is whether this compressed latent buys anything downstream. four claims, each against a strong baseline:

| claim | statement | baseline | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | z as additional policy input improves success | same policy without z, matched budget | no, three design generations, best case parity, typical case harm |
| b | z replaces vision at ~70x fewer tokens | dense patch policy at parity | no at 200 demos, 0/50 rollouts twice. only the sub 100 demo regime is untested |
| f | force fused in the encoder beats raw force wired into the policy | vision + raw windowed force | blocked at rung 0, raw force itself is null on both tested tasks, so the fusion arm never earned a build |
| c | z is the better substrate for latent dynamics and planning | dynamics over dense patch tokens | open, untested by us, now the primary axis |

## what has been run, with results

**v0.1, rh20t pretrain.** one encoder across 4 embodiments matches per robot specialists in domain and dominates off diagonal (5 seed transfer matrix). vision only z beats raw vit features on the force/ee probe on every force sensored robot (+0.04 to +0.08 r2). force is the distinctive cross modal signal, joint angles are mostly recoverable from vision anyway.

**v0.2, multi camera + predictor.** multicam fusion holds rank (rankme 153 vs 136) and improves the force probe (0.283 vs 0.251). a frozen encoder plus small predictor beats latent carry forward by 28 to 36 percent per horizon (replicated in sim at 11 to 19 percent). two negative findings that shaped everything after: the temporal in encoder variant failed its gate twice (rankme 51 vs 134, future force probe 0.10 vs 0.17, time pooling dilutes per frame signal), and the fused embedding is view specific, same instant different camera compositions sit 1.46x further apart than same composition across instants, with early fusion > best single view > late fusion / per view averaging.

**first downstream test (sim pick and place, diffusion policy, 500 demos, 2 seeds, matched checkpoints).** z as replacement input: 14 percent (2 percent with pretrained init) vs baseline 32. z concatenated next to the resnet stream: flat 24 to 28 vs baseline plateau 32 to 36. the action readout probe explains it: fused z 0.335 vs raw patches 0.413 r2 on commanded action sequences, the state prediction objective keeps no object detail. data parity was airtight and the encoder even had extra in domain pretraining, which makes the null stronger.

**v0.3, objective fix then insertion sweep.** added masked per patch feature reconstruction pressure. every offline gate improved, short horizon geometry dse3@0.25s 0.327 to 0.414 (raw 0.49), action readout 0.573 to 0.620, mutual knn 0.602 to 0.655. query count ablated twice: q8 = q32 = q64 under the old objective, q8 > q32 > q64 under the detail objective (0.414 / 0.368 / 0.344), capacity spreads detail instead of packing it. then the downstream sweep:

| arm | result | read |
|---|---|---|
| concat rerun with the better v0.3 encoder | 16 vs 22 and 8 vs 32 at matched ep50 | probe improvements inverted downstream, offline better, rollouts worse |
| future latent alignment aux loss on the policy, weight sweep 0.2 / 0.05 / 0.01 / 0 | 4 / 10-14 / 26 / 24-26 percent | monotone dose response harm. frozen target with an irreducible cosine floor, gradient never anneals, distorts obs features invisibly to train mse |
| unpooled 8 tokens | ep50 10 percent, inside the 8-32 dense band | pooling was not the mistake |

| force rung 0, square | 66/72 to 54/54 | square is vision saturated |

also resolved here: an earlier readout ordering that had raw beating z was a train/test split artifact (frame level split leaks near duplicates and flatters the 9216 d representation). under clean episode held out splits z carries the action information. the binding constraint was fast fine geometry, not linear task information.

**v0.4 wave 1, state as query on the clean substrate (robomimic square, 200 proficient human demos, fast eval ddpm-16 x 50 rollouts, gates preregistered before any number existed).** the new encoder injects state into the cross attention queries through zero init tanh gates, alpha 0 equivalence to the v0.3 encoder verified bit identical, 4/4 conditioned/unconditioned query split, per dim state dropout, gaussian state noise, annealed condition dropout, and offline shortcut detectors (d1 null condition sensitivity, d2 state to z ridge r2). all arms detector clean before rollouts. ep50 board:

claims a, b, f all no on square, per the preregistered gates. the one positive: v0.3 encoder content reaches chassis parity when inserted as gated tokens, so the earlier downstream failures are fully attributed to insertion mode plus task saturation plus benchmark noise, not encoder quality.

## what we think the problem is

**1. the consumption model was wrong, not the encoder.** the verified literature agrees with our data on every point. frozen pretrained encoders lose to end to end vision by 42 percent on average at 160 to 1000 demos, and every published frozen encoder win sits at or below 100 demos (r3m 5-25, dmc-vb at 1 percent data, ladi-wm at 10 demos). "extra tokens beside a trainable vision stack" has zero published wins at any demo count, and our concat collapse has an external replication, injecting point cloud tokens into a token native vla drops 73.2 to 18.6 on rlbench. the one insertion pattern with a published win is pull side, action tokens cross attending the new modality inside the action head (82.3 vs 73.2 vs 18.6 in the same paper), which we never tested.

**2. three distinct harm mechanisms, all with normal looking training.** concat corrupts the policys observation distribution at rollout while train loss stays normal, the classic compounding covariate shift signature. state conditioned extraction creates policy time input non stationarity, elevated train loss with open gates and clean shortcut detectors, the tokens are genuinely harder to fit. and unchaseable aux targets, a frozen target with a cosine floor never anneals, so its gradient permanently distorts the observation encoder in proportion to weight. published positives for the aux mechanism (frappe) put the loss on dedicated prefix tokens at a deep layer with the dose response we failed to get on the obs encoder, placement is the variable, and discrete cross entropy targets beat continuous cosine in every ablated comparison.

**3. offline metrics decouple from closed loop success, now externally corroborated four ways.** our three internal inversions (better probes, worse rollouts) match: best val loss checkpoints 50-100 percent worse than best rollout checkpoints, shortcut arms getting lower val loss at 3 vs 53 percent success, "predictive but not plannable" world models, and encoders as decision makers rather than interchangeable feature extractors. the intact paper (2607.26056) adds the one exception worth testing: a predicted vs expert action family metric (small action head, knn/cka neighborhood overlap) tracks closed loop success at r 0.90-0.95 across 45 checkpoints, while pointwise action r2 is weaker and effective rank inverts (higher rank, lower success). that metric is calibratable on our existing evaluated checkpoints with zero rollouts. expectation registered in advance: an encoder side metric can rank encoders but never insertion modes, our pooled and concat arms share one encoder and score 0.82 vs 0.34.

**4. the objective family has a structural failure mode we now screen for.** a reproducible result (the "obsessed encoder" experiments, code public) shows jepa style objectives including our exact recipe family collapse capacity onto any low entropy channel that is constant within a view or window and predictable across samples, a 12 bit static pattern comes to dominate a 1024 d latent while sigreg stays satisfied and the collapsed run gets lower loss than the clean run. diversity regularizers cannot prevent this, the collapsing model already carries one, and clustering based regularization collapsed in the same experiments. our measured view specificity (1.46x) is this failure family in the wild, camera identity is a static predictable channel. pmae (2502.06314) gives the variance side twin: continuous reconstruction style losses weight dimensions by variance, and task relevant signal lives in low variance components that barely move the loss, which is a clean mechanism for why discrete cross entropy targets beat continuous cosine, and why our continuous cosine aux failed. consequence for any future encoder training: nuisance dominance probes on cached z (camera id, episode id, variance decomposition), a loss crossover monitor (pretrain loss below the control sibling is a red flag, never a win), a static pattern canary for new objective families, and the standing rule that a future predictor must beat latent carry forward, since a collapsed predictor equals carry by definition.

**5. what is genuinely still open.** three axes, each with published support our evaluations were structurally blind to. distribution shift robustness: pretrained representation advantages appear only out of distribution (in distribution parity says nothing, mvp goes 39.9 to 6.6 under shift), sota manipulation policies lose 30-50 percent to single axis perturbations, and task driven compressed representations beat dense rgb under combined shift (0.45/0.70 vs 0.00) with the mechanism being task driven selection of what to compress, which is what our action readout and future latent auxiliaries do. the sub 100 demo regime: the only place frozen representations ever win, directly below our 200 demo operating point, robomimic ships the subset protocol. and compact latent dynamics for planning: 8 token planning runs 37x cheaper (compact, fsq tokens, near parity with a 784 token latent) to 130x cheaper (reachability conditioned aux fixes plannability, dense baseline ooms at 1024 candidates), amortizable to 2.9-5.5 ms per intent versus 1.48 s cem, and the decisive comparison, compressed token set versus dense patch tokens as world model state in manipulation, is unpublished. dino-wm dense beats pooled (0.90 vs 0.44) is the headwind, but it pooled spatially rather than using a query token set, the question is open.

**encoder rebuild (parallel track, does not gate the above).** windowed multi modal encoder, strict batch x time x sensors x features contract with one code path including t=1, per modality conv1d over time stem (local motion context before spatial compression), spatial compress then temporal resample to a chosen output rate r (default 16), fusion keeps [r, 3 x n_s] tokens with modality and slot embeddings and joint self attention instead of flatten to a single linear (the flatten was a single lossy 6144 to 256 cross modal contact point), hide one modality via attention mask rather than zero slicing (zeroed slices leak a learnable hidden symbol through biases), no mla (no kv cache exists in a single bidirectional pass at any r). one open config: sum modalities after alignment instead of cross modal attention, screened as a flag, the token variants attention maps answer whether cross modal attention was needed at all. preregistered gates: the temporal z must beat per frame z on future force and short horizon geometry probes (the exact gate the v0.2 temporal encoder failed), a static pattern canary plus loss crossover watch in the smoke battery, and screening economics preserved (2-4h pretrains). purpose: substrate for claim c and for a real multi rate force arm if force ever passes rung 0. this rebuild does not reopen claims a or b.

**objective candidates for a later wave (not the current rebuild):** action grounded terms inside pretraining (action nll on displacement intents, dynamics alignment, both now triangulated by intact + mcr as the terms that track control), and variance normalized or discrete cross entropy latent targets per the pmae mechanism. side note, best of k exploration training (2607.27372) claims 1 step diffusion policy inference at parity, watch list only, policy training method changes are out of scope for this program.

## what we will not rerun (falsified, with evidence)

obs side token insertion at >= 200 demos (3 internal generations + external replication + zero published wins). z only replacing vision at >= 200 demos (0/50 twice, frozen backbone minus 42 percent). aux loss distillation into the policy obs encoder (monotone dose response). raw force without a curriculum (rung 0 null on both tasks, the FACTR confound is on record: naive policies ignore force, so the null reads "naive wiring does not help", not "force is useless"). offline probes as go gates (kill only, permanently). efficiency claims based on policy side token count (decode dominates latency, unanimous across 4 sources, the surviving efficiency story is planner call cost). diversity or rank regularizers and clustering as collapse fixes (both collapsed in controlled experiments, rank anti correlates with success in intact). a new escalation task before the shift battery has run.
